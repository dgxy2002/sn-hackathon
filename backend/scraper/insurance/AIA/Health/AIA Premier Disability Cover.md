Scenario

Jack Tan is a 34-year-old sales executive who is married with one child. As the
family’s main income earner, he decides to protect his future earnings with
AIA Premier Disability Cover. He applies for the protection level of 50% of his
existing income of S$8,000 per month (S$4,000 monthly benefit), at an annual
premium of S$1,376.

At the age of 45, he suffers a stroke that leaves him with significant speech impairment. He is
unable to communicate effectively with his clients and continue working as a sales executive.
He receives sick pay during the 60-day deferment period. Following that, Jack will receive the
monthly benefit of S$4,000 for the next 2 years as his disability prevents him from going back to
work. His future premium payments are waived, but he continues to be covered under the plan.
On his doctor’s recommendation, and approved in advance by AIA, he undergoes speech therapy
and is reimbursed for the full cost of S$16,800 under the rehabilitation benefit.

After claiming 2 years of benefits, his condition improves but he is still unable to work as a sales
executive. He is also unable to work in a suitable occupation that he has been taught, experienced
or trained for, so he pivots to a part-time office cleaner earning S$1,500 per month. As long as his
disability persists or up to the age of 65, whichever is earlier, he will receive a monthly benefit of
S$2,500, thereby topping up his total income to the value of his S$4,000 monthly benefit.
